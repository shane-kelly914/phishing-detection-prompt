Phishing Analysis Report
1. Risk Level: High
2. Reason for Risk Level: This message impersonates FedEx using a clearly fraudulent domain, employs a generic greeting, manufactures urgency with a 48-hour deadline, and directs the recipient to an unofficial link to "confirm shipping details." Package delivery scams are one of the most common and successful phishing categories, particularly because recipients often have legitimate packages in transit and may click without scrutiny.
3. Phishing Indicators Identified:

Impersonation of a trusted entity (FedEx)
Suspicious/mismatched domain
Urgency and time pressure (48-hour deadline)
Generic greeting lacking personalization
Unexpected request to click a link and provide information
Threat-based pressure (package returned to sender)
No tracking number or order-specific details

4. Summary: The message claims to be from FedEx, stating that a delivery attempt failed due to an address problem and pressuring the recipient to click a link within 48 hours to update shipping details or risk having the package returned. The link points to a non-FedEx domain designed to look official.
5. Red Flags:

The domain fedex-delivery-update-help.net is not a legitimate FedEx domain — FedEx uses fedex.com exclusively for customer-facing communications
The domain stacks descriptive keywords ("delivery," "update," "help") — a hallmark of phishing infrastructure designed to appear trustworthy
The .net TLD with hyphens is inconsistent with how a major corporation structures its official URLs
"Hello" is a generic greeting — real FedEx notifications typically reference the recipient's name and account
No tracking number is provided — legitimate FedEx delivery exception notices always include a specific tracking number, sender information, and delivery address details
No specific package details (sender, contents, expected delivery date) are mentioned, indicating a mass-distributed template
Artificial 48-hour deadline designed to rush the recipient past careful thought
Threat of consequence (return to sender) leverages mild fear and inconvenience
Signed vaguely as "FedEx Delivery Team" rather than from a specific FedEx notification address

6. Legitimate Signals: The message is grammatically clean and uses plausible language for a delivery notification. However, well-written phishing is now standard, and the absence of any verifiable details (tracking number, address, sender) outweighs surface-level polish.
7. Confidence Level: High
The fraudulent domain alone is sufficient to classify this as phishing. Combined with the missing tracking number, generic greeting, and urgency tactics, this is a textbook example of a delivery-impersonation scam.
8. Recommended Action:

Do not click the link — it almost certainly leads to a credential-harvesting page or a form requesting personal and payment information (some delivery scams charge a small "redelivery fee" to capture card details)
Do not enter any personal, address, or payment information if the link was already clicked
If expecting a real FedEx package, go directly to fedex.com and enter the tracking number from the original shipping confirmation
Report the message as phishing through the email client's built-in reporting tool
Forward suspected FedEx phishing to FedEx's abuse address: abuse@fedex.com
Delete the message after reporting
If any information was already submitted through the link, monitor financial accounts closely and consider placing a fraud alert with credit bureaus
