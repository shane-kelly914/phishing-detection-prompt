Phishing Analysis Report
1. Risk Level: Low
2. Reason for Risk Level: This message exhibits the characteristics of a normal, routine workplace request. It uses a personalized greeting, contains no urgency, requests no sensitive information, includes no links or attachments, and has a casual, plausible business context. There are no manipulation tactics or impersonation red flags evident in the message body itself.
3. Phishing Indicators Identified: None significant. The message lacks the typical hallmarks of phishing — no urgency, no credential requests, no suspicious links, no emotional manipulation, no fear or reward tactics.
4. Summary: The message appears to be a casual internal request from a coworker named Mark asking Shane for an updated copy of a client proposal. The tone is informal and considerate ("No rush if you're busy"), consistent with everyday colleague-to-colleague communication.
5. Red Flags:

The sender's full email address is not visible in the provided content, so domain spoofing cannot be fully ruled out without verification
"Quick question" is a generic subject line sometimes used in business email compromise (BEC) attempts, though it's also extremely common in legitimate correspondence
The request is for a document (client proposal) that could contain sensitive business information if the sender is impersonating a real coworker

6. Legitimate Signals:

Personalized greeting using the recipient's name ("Hey Shane")
Signed with a name ("Mark") suggesting a known colleague
No urgency or pressure tactics
No requests for credentials, payments, codes, or personal information
No links or attachments
Natural, grammatically correct language
Polite, low-pressure tone ("No rush if you're busy")
Plausible business context (requesting an updated document)

7. Confidence Level: Medium
Confidence is medium rather than high because phishing analysis of an email body alone is inherently limited. BEC attacks specifically mimic this exact style — short, casual, low-pressure messages from "coworkers" — and the sender's actual email address, headers, and reply-to fields are not available for inspection.
8. Recommended Action:
Treat as likely legitimate, but verify before sending the proposal, especially if the document contains sensitive client information. Specifically:

Confirm the sender's email address matches Mark's known internal address (watch for lookalike domains or display-name spoofing)
If anything about the address looks off, verify with Mark through a separate channel (Slack, phone, or in person) before sending
If the proposal contains confidential client data, verifying out-of-band is good practice regardless
No need to report or block — proceed with normal caution
